@@rec id=CE-01 tier=core_essential items=1
### Make antibiotic stewardship a formal leadership priority
Work collaboratively with the healthcare facility management/leadership to formally prioritize antibiotic stewardship and secure organizational support.

@@rec id=CE-02 tier=core_essential items=2
### Include stewardship and KPIs in the facility annual plan
Integrate antibiotic stewardship activities into healthcare facility annual plans and include key performance indicators.

@@rec id=CE-03 tier=core_essential items=3
### Formalize an antibiotic stewardship committee
Formalize an antibiotic stewardship committee that is tasked with reviewing policies, procedures, treatment guidelines, and operational considerations related to antibiotic stewardship.

@@rec id=CE-04 tier=core_essential items=4@0-2.5
### Form a multidisciplinary stewardship committee
Form a multidisciplinary antibiotic stewardship committee that includes 1–7 of the healthcare workers listed here: infection prevention and control (IPC) physician(s), IPC nurse(s), non-IPC nurse(s), infectious diseases trained physician(s) or clinician(s) with experience practicing infectious diseases, intensive care unit physician(s), surgeon, general medicine physician(s), other physician(s), infectious diseases trained pharmacist(s) or pharmacist with experience practicing infectious diseases, other clinical pharmacist(s), other staff pharmacist(s), healthcare facility leader(s), clinical microbiologist(s), information technology specialist(s).
<!-- SME note: Tier 1 for selection of 1–7 of the listed healthcare workers. -->

@@rec id=CE-05 tier=core_essential items=5
### Set a regular committee meeting schedule
Organize a regular meeting schedule (e.g., monthly, bimonthly, quarterly) to convene the members of the antibiotic stewardship committee.

@@rec id=CE-06C tier=core_essential items=6C
### Formalize the stewardship team and designate a leader
Formalize an antibiotic stewardship team that is responsible for conducting daily antibiotic stewardship activities. Designate at least one leader of the antibiotic stewardship team. This is typically an infectious diseases trained physician (or clinician(s) with experience practicing infectious diseases). For healthcare facilities that have access to an infectious diseases trained pharmacist(s) (or clinical pharmacist with experience practicing infectious diseases), designate him/her as the co-lead of the program.

@@rec id=CE-07 tier=core_essential items=7
### Set a regular team meeting schedule
Organize a regular meeting schedule (e.g., daily, weekly) to convene the members of the antibiotic stewardship team.

@@rec id=CE-08 tier=core_essential items=8
### Give the committee or team decision-making authority
Develop a hospital policy to authorize the antibiotic stewardship committee or team to make decisions about policies or procedures related to antibiotic use.

@@rec id=CE-09 tier=core_essential items=9
### Collaborate with key departments and teams
Collaborate with the following hospital departments or teams to implement antibiotic stewardship initiatives: infection prevention and control, infectious diseases, patient safety, quality, pharmacy, microbiology, drug and therapeutics committee, HIV/tuberculosis (TB) team, and surgery or operating theater.

@@rec id=CE-11 tier=core_essential items=11
### Involve stewardship expertise in formulary and procurement decisions
Create a process to update the antibiotic formulary and/or make antibiotic procurement decisions that involves at least an infectious diseases trained physician or clinician with experience practicing infectious diseases, infectious diseases trained pharmacist(s) or other clinical pharmacist(s) or other staff pharmacist(s) (if pharmacists are present at HCF), and a clinical microbiologist (if microbiologist is present at HCF).

@@rec id=CE-12 tier=core_essential items=12
### Evaluate evidence before adding new antibiotics to the formulary
Create a process to review evidence related to the safety, efficacy, and cost of new antibiotics before adding to the formulary.

@@rec id=CE-16 tier=core_essential items=16
### Provide basic equipment for the stewardship team
Acquire basic equipment (e.g., telephone, computer) to perform antibiotic stewardship activities.

@@rec id=CE-20 tier=core_essential items=20
### Provide access to current peer-reviewed evidence
Obtain access to updated evidence in the form of peer-reviewed scientific literature (e.g., published research).

@@rec id=CE-26 tier=core_essential items=26
### Add stewardship to staff induction training
Integrate antibiotic stewardship topics (e.g., optimizing antibiotic use) in staff induction training (e.g., new-hire training) for healthcare workers with clinical roles and responsibilities (e.g., nurses, pharmacists, physicians).
<!-- SME note: Revise to make the target audience of education/training more specific. -->

@@rec id=CE-29 tier=core_essential items=29
### Train the stewardship team
Ensure the antibiotic stewardship team is adequately trained to perform daily activities. Request access to continuing education opportunities to maintain knowledge and skills on antibiotic stewardship and IPC related topics.

@@rec id=CE-31 tier=core_essential items=31
### Develop infectious diseases treatment guidelines
Develop infectious diseases treatment guidelines to guide diagnostic and antibiotic use. Adapt existing resources (e.g., national clinical practice guidelines) to local healthcare facility context. Consider developing guidelines for the following infectious diseases: urinary tract infection, community-acquired pneumonia, hospital-acquired pneumonia, ventilator-associated pneumonia, sepsis, skin and soft tissue infection, surgical site infection, central line-associated bloodstream infection, surgical prophylaxis, intra-abdominal infection, febrile neutropenia, multidrug-resistant organisms, bacterial meningitis, and infective endocarditis.
<!-- SME note: Recommend using existing resources to help draft. -->

@@rec id=CE-32 tier=core_essential items=32
### Include essential elements in every treatment guideline
Include the following elements in all infectious diseases treatment guidelines: first-line antibiotic agent, dose, duration, and alternative antibiotic agents (e.g., penicillin allergy, pregnant women, oral antibiotics). Consider also categorizing recommended antibiotics by WHO AWaRe classification.

@@rec id=CE-33 tier=core_essential items=33
### Review and update treatment guidelines periodically
Create a process to review and update infectious diseases treatment guidelines periodically based on availability of new evidence and communicate any changes to providers.

@@rec id=CE-34 tier=core_essential items=34
### Use the facility antibiogram to inform treatment guidelines
Use the healthcare facility antibiogram to guide selection of a first-line empiric antibiotic and alternatives when developing the infectious diseases treatment guidelines. Modify recommendations based on significant changes in antibiotic susceptibility patterns.

@@rec id=CE-35 tier=core_essential items=35:DJL
### Implement core stewardship interventions
Consider implementation of the following antibiotic stewardship actions:
- **D.** Intravenous to oral antibiotic formulation conversion
- **J.** Assessment and clarification of documented antibiotic allergies
- **L.** Alerts to prescribers about potentially duplicative antibiotic coverage (e.g., double anti-anaerobic coverage)

@@rec id=CE-36 tier=core_essential items=36
### Develop policies or SOPs for stewardship activities
Develop hospital policies and/or standardized operating procedures for relevant antibiotic stewardship activities (e.g., audit and feedback, guideline development, diagnostic testing protocols).

@@rec id=CS-18 tier=core_standard items=18

Establish antibiotic stewardship team access to electronic medical record, list of antibiotics purchased, list of antibiotics dispensed, antibiotic administration records, syndromic antibiogram (e.g., antibiogram with urine cultures), cumulative antibiogram (defined as report that shows the susceptibility of commonly isolated organisms to antibiotics in a defined period of time).

@@rec id=CS-35 tier=core_standard items=35:EGMOR
### Implement standard stewardship interventions
Consider implementation of the following antibiotic stewardship actions:
- **E.** Prospective audit and feedback of specified antibiotics: review of courses of therapy for these antibiotics to be evaluated for appropriateness (e.g., concordance with treatment guidelines), followed by regular feedback on use of the agent to the prescribing clinician
- **G.** Restricted use of antibiotics based on formulary approval for prespecified conditions or populations: clinicians are unable to prescribe certain antibiotics to certain types of patients
- **M.** Alerts to prescribers about drug-drug interactions with antimicrobials
- **O.** Management of antibiotic shortages/stockouts (communicate shortages/stockouts and develop plan of action such as recommendation of alternative agents)
- **R.** Awareness campaigns on responsible use of antibiotics
<!-- SME note: drug-drug interactions — add "with antibiotic"; add more detail to "manage antibiotic shortages" (communicate and develop action plan). -->

@@rec id=CS-37 tier=core_standard items=37
### Produce a stewardship activity report
Develop a report on antibiotic stewardship activities and include the following: performance against process and outcome indicators for antibiotic use, antibiotic appropriateness, antibiotic resistance (e.g., aggregate susceptibility reports for specific pathogens), key areas of improvement, areas for further improvement or priority, and areas in which guidance or support from executive and governance units is needed.

@@rec id=CS-47 tier=core_standard items=47 needs=micro
### Ensure timely culture and susceptibility reporting
Establish a process to ensure that the clinical microbiology laboratory provides culture and susceptibility results to prescribers in a timely manner (e.g., within 72 hours of specimen collection).

@@rec id=CS-52 tier=core_standard items=52
### Conduct prescription audits or point prevalence surveys
Conduct prescription audits or point prevalence surveys to assess the appropriateness of antibiotic prescribing.

@@rec id=CS-53 tier=core_standard items=53 needs=ehr
### Monitor and report antibiotic use
Conduct routine monitoring and reporting of the quantity and types of antibiotics used (purchased, prescribed, or dispensed).

@@rec id=CS-54 tier=core_standard items=54 needs=ehr
### Use a standardized antibiotic use metric
Measure antibiotic use or consumption using a standardized metric such as days of therapy or defined daily doses.

@@rec id=CS-55 tier=core_standard items=55
### Develop action plans for identified problems
Develop action plans to address the problems identified related to optimization of antibiotic use (e.g., increase in the consumption of broad-spectrum antibiotics).

@@rec id=CS-58 tier=core_standard items=58
### Monitor impact metrics
Consider use of the following metrics to assess impact of antibiotic stewardship activities: antibiotic use or consumption, antibiotic appropriateness (agent, dose, duration), time to appropriate antibiotic therapy, cost-savings, in-hospital mortality, length of stay, Clostridioides difficile infection rates, rehospitalization, antibiotic-related adverse events, antibiotic-related near misses (e.g., an error that could have led to an adverse event but did not result in clinical harm), and antibiotic costs (e.g., purchase price and expenditure).

@@rec id=CS-63 tier=core_standard items=63 needs=micro
### Monitor and report resistance for priority pathogens
Monitor antibiotic susceptibility data and report resistance rates for priority pathogens.

@@rec id=CS-66 tier=core_standard items=66 needs=micro
### Develop and update a facility antibiogram
Develop and aggregate a facility antibiogram and regularly update it (e.g., annually).

@@rec id=AE-35 tier=advanced_enhancement items=35:ABCFHIKNQ
### Implement advanced stewardship interventions
Consider implementation of the following antibiotic stewardship actions:
- **A.** Antibiotic automatic stops (e.g., 24 hours for surgical prophylaxis): antibiotics are stopped automatically after a predefined time period according to indication regardless of physician order
- **B.** Antibiotic "time outs" (e.g., at 48 hours a clinician is prompted to review any empiric IV antibiotic therapy): defined, regular prompts to the clinician to re-evaluate antibiotic choices
- **C.** Antibiotic reminders: alerts at the time of prescribing that are tied to a particular antibiotic
- **F.** Prior authorization of specified antibiotics: requires clinicians to obtain timely approval from experts (e.g., trained physicians or pharmacists) before these antibiotics get dispensed
- **H.** Peer comparisons on antibiotic prescribing: reports including individual-level antibiotic prescribing for all clinicians within a predefined group are developed and shared
- **I.** In-person antibiotic stewardship clinical rounds: rounds held on a regular basis to review and discuss antibiotic choices and ensure accordance with best practice guidelines
- **K.** Review of outpatient parenteral antibiotic therapy prior to discharge
- **N.** Dose optimization based on pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic parameters for treatment of organisms with reduced antibiotic susceptibility
- **Q.** Therapeutic drug monitoring of antibiotics with narrow therapeutic index (e.g., vancomycin, aminoglycosides)
